Data deletion instructions

Ore & Tar social media platform · Last updated 28 September 2026

This page explains how to remove the Ore & Tar platform's access to your social accounts and how to have the data we hold about you deleted. What we collect and why is set out in our privacy policy.

1. Remove our access to your accounts

Either of these stops the Service from reading or publishing to your account straight away.

  • In the Service: open the brand's Settings, then Channels, and choose Disconnect. The stored access token is destroyed at once.
  • Facebook and Instagram: in Facebook, go to Settings & privacy, then Settings, then Business integrations, and remove Ore & Tar. In Instagram, go to Settings, then Apps and websites, and remove it.
  • LinkedIn: go to Settings, then Data privacy, then Permitted services, and remove Ore & Tar.

2. Ask us to delete your data

Email manenji@oreandtar.com with the subject "Data deletion request" and tell us:

  • whether you use the Service, manage a connected account, or commented on or clicked a link in a post published through it;
  • the account, Page or profile concerned (a link is enough).

We may ask you to confirm that the account is yours before we act. We handle each request individually, confirm receipt within 5 working days and complete the deletion within 30 days.

What is deleted

  • If you use the Service: your name and email address are removed and your user record is anonymised.
  • If you manage a connected account: its access token, and on request the content, performance data and comments held for it.
  • If you commented on a post: the text, name or handle and hashed identifier stored for your comments.

Link clicks are stored only as one-way hashes that we cannot look up from your name or account, so we cannot find them from your request. Posts already published stay on the platform until they are deleted: on the brand's instruction we can delete them from Facebook, LinkedIn and X, and the account's owner can always delete them there. Instagram posts must be deleted on Instagram.

After a deletion we keep the record of who did what (the audit log) and evidence of what was approved for publication, as our privacy policy explains. These contain identifiers, not your content. Deleted data also leaves our database backups when those backups expire. If a backup is restored before then, your deletion is applied again.
